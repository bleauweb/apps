Our apps on the Mac

The same apps as on iPhone and iPad, built as real Mac apps and handed over directly from here. No App Store, no account, no installer that wants your password.

Each one is signed with our Developer ID and notarised by Apple, which means macOS has checked it and will open it without argument. You drag it to your Applications folder and that is the whole installation. Nothing runs in the background, nothing adds a login item, and deleting the app deletes the app.

Everything on this page needs macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later and runs natively on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Installing one

  1. Open the disk image you downloaded. A window appears with the app on the left and your Applications folder on the right.
  2. Drag the app onto Applications. That is the installation.
  3. Eject the disk image, and open the app from Launchpad, Spotlight or your Applications folder.

The first time you open it, macOS will ask whether the app may see your Calendar and Reminders — or, for the others, nothing at all. Say no and the app explains what it cannot do rather than pretending to work; you can change your mind later in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

Checking what you downloaded

Every download here lists a SHA-256 checksum, and you are welcome to confirm it before opening anything. In Terminal:

shasum -a 256 ~/Downloads/TheFile.dmg

If the line it prints matches the one on this page, the file arrived exactly as it left. If it does not, do not open it — tell us instead.

Why not the Mac App Store?

These are small apps made by one person, and the Mac App Store adds a review queue between fixing something and you having the fix. Handing the file over directly keeps that short. It costs you one extra step — dragging the app to Applications — and costs us the obligation to be plain about what you are installing, which is the trade we would rather make.

The iPhone and iPad versions do go through the App Store, because on those platforms there is no other way to install anything.

Uninstalling

Drag the app to the Trash. What it made — your entries, your plans, your progress — lives in your own Calendar, Reminders and iCloud, not inside the app, so removing the app leaves all of it exactly where it was. Nothing else is left behind, because nothing else was ever put anywhere.
