Support

Every question is welcome, including the ones that feel too small to ask.

The fastest way to get help is email: support@bleauweb.org. Describe what happened in your own words — screenshots help if they are easy for you, and are never required.

Questions, answered

Where is my data stored?

In your own Apple services, nowhere else. Entries are real events and reminders in your Calendar and Reminders. The details Apple's apps have no place for — energy, steps, categories, which days a repeat was carried or let go of — live on your device and sync through the private iCloud attached to your Apple Account. There is no NeuRoutine server and no account to create. The full story is the privacy policy.

Why does the app ask for full access to Calendar and Reminders?

Because that is where your plans are stored — asking is the honest price of keeping your data in your own apps instead of somewhere private to us. NeuRoutine writes only into the calendar and list it creates for itself; everything else it can see is read-only, and only if you switch it on in Settings.

Why don't my work meetings (or birthdays, or holidays) show up?

By choice, NeuRoutine starts by showing only its own calendar and its own list. A day that opens onto every meeting, feed and birthday the phone knows about is the wall the app exists to avoid. Any calendar can be added in Settings → Calendars — one switch each — and its entries then count on your day. They stay editable where they came from, but you can give them an energy number.

Does NeuRoutine send notifications?

No — never, and on purpose. No alerts, no badges, no reminders to do things, and it never asks for notification permission. The app shows you your day when you open it and is silent when you don't.

What does the energy number mean?

What something will take out of you, on a deliberately small scale: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13. The gaps widen as the numbers grow because nobody can tell a 6 from a 7 — and being asked to is its own small tax. Zero is a real answer ("has to happen, costs nothing"), and so is leaving something unsized.

What are categories for?

Keeping work things and home things apart, or whatever your days actually divide into. Everything starts in Todo; add your own in Settings → Categories and pick which one new entries take by default. You are never asked to choose one while writing something down — capture stays two fields — and any entry can be re-filed later from its Edit details. The Energy tab totals each category over the window you pick, so "where did it all go?" has an answer.

Why can't I change the daily budget of 13?

Because a dial there would get turned up on exactly the day it should not be — the week that is already too much is the week you decide you can take fifteen. A day asking more than 13 says so in a sentence; that is a fact about the day, not about you.

What if I don't do a repeating thing one day?

Open it and choose Skip just today. That is not a missed day and not a completed one: the day costs nothing, nothing counts it as carried, and the routine arrives tomorrow exactly as it would have. It is reversible — the same button offers to put the day back. If a whole day ends up let go of, the app says so plainly rather than congratulating you: a decision, not a failure.

Can I move something without deleting it?

Yes — open it, and under Not right now: move it to tomorrow, to the end of the day, or back to the inbox. (A repeat gets Skip just today instead of tomorrow, because its day is the anchor its whole series counts from — moving it would move every occurrence.) Plain buttons, not a hidden swipe, and every one of them can be undone.

What does "low energy day" do?

It is you telling the app how today is, and the app answering with a shorter list. Flip the switch on Now and Now shows only what you could manage; you set where that line sits in Settings → Low energy days — keep things costing at most a number you choose, and whether unsized things and anything marked High stay regardless.

It is on until tomorrow and then gone, with nothing to remember to undo. Three things it never does: hide anything with a time on it (a 14:00 appointment happens whether the day is heavy or not), change The Plan (which always shows the whole day), or change what the day costs — the meter and the Energy tab do not move, because saying the day is hard does not make it cheaper. Nothing is completed, skipped or moved.

Can I edit entries in Apple's Calendar and Reminders?

Yes — they are ordinary events and reminders, and edits made there show up in NeuRoutine on their own. Completing a repeating reminder in Apple's Reminders counts here too: the day you carried it is recorded. One nuance: a repeat rule NeuRoutine cannot express (say, every second Tuesday) is shown faithfully but edited only in Apple's apps — the app will never rewrite a rule it does not fully understand.

Why does a dragged entry sometimes spring back?

The day never runs backwards in time: a morning task cannot be placed after an afternoon appointment, because 14:00 does not come before 09:00. Anything without a time can go anywhere. The row returning is the app saying "not there" without a dialog to dismiss.

How do I set up sync?

You don't — being signed in to iCloud is the whole setup. Entries travel with your calendars, details travel through your private iCloud, and everything keeps working offline in the meantime. Settings → iCloud in the app shows where things currently live.

How do I delete my data?

Delete entries in the app (behind Edit, deliberately) or in Apple's apps. Deleting the app removes everything on the device; the synced details can be removed from iCloud under Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Account Storage. Nothing exists anywhere else to delete.
