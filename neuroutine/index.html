A planner that stays calm

NeuRoutine is a low-friction planner for iPhone and iPad, designed neurodivergent-friendly first: one thing at a time, energy on an honest scale, and no interruptions — ever.

Everything you plan stays in your own Calendar, Reminders and iCloud. No account, no server, and the app never notifies.

One thing at a time

Today leads with the single thing that is up now — with its next small step and one Done button. Underneath, one meter with one meaning: dim is still to carry, solid is already carried, always against the same fixed 13.

The full month exists, but it is never the front door. A wall of thirty-five days is exactly what this app is built not to hand you.

Energy, not just time

Anything can carry an energy number — 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 or 13 — and every day has the same fixed budget of 13. The meter reports what the day asks and what is left; it never tells you what to do about it.

The Energy tab looks backwards, too: which days were heavy, and which weekday keeps being the heaviest. Patterns become visible without anyone scoring you. A day that asked for more than 13 is named as a fact about the day — not about you.

On the Home Screen, without the noise

The On deck widget shows the one thing to do now, what follows it, and a Done button that works right from the Home Screen. No counts, no lists, no red badges.

Two Lock Screen rings keep the day's two facts apart — how much of the list is done, and how much energy is left — because a day can be four-of-five done and still ask for more than there is. And a Control Centre button opens the app straight into capture, cursor already in the field.

Your data stays yours

Entries are real events and reminders in your own Calendar and Reminders — usable in Apple's apps, on every device you already have. The details Apple's apps have no place for sync through your private iCloud. No account, no server, nothing collected, and nothing to set up.

The privacy policy is one page, because there is almost nothing to say.

The rest of the argument

Big things break down

Any entry can hold small steps, and the focus card always names the next one. Capture asks exactly two things — what it is, and what it will cost — because every extra decision is a reason not to write the thing down at all.

It never interrupts

No notifications, no badges, no nudges about what is still undone — it never even asks for permission to notify. The app shows you your day when you open it, and is silent when you don't.

Predictable on purpose

Weeks always start on Monday, the month grid never changes shape, one colour carries one meaning, and a day can be dragged into your order — with the one rule that the clock never runs backwards.

Made to be spoken to

Siri shortcuts add something, tick something off for the right day, or say how today is going — and full VoiceOver support, Dynamic Type through the accessibility sizes, and honest tap targets are part of the app, not an afterthought.
