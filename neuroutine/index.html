A planner that stays calm

NeuRoutine is a low-friction planner for iPhone and iPad, designed neurodivergent-friendly first: one thing at a time, energy on an honest scale, and no interruptions — ever.

The app opens on Now: the single thing that is up, one Done button, and a quiet "then:" underneath. Arranging the day, the week and the month lives next door in The Plan — and the month is never the front door.

One thing at a time

The Now tab leads with the single thing that is up — and, when that thing breaks into steps, the steps sit right underneath it, tickable where you are already looking. Breaking a daunting thing down is worth little if carrying out a piece means navigating away from it.

Then one meter with one meaning: dim is still to carry, solid is already carried, always against the same fixed 13.

Energy, not just time

Anything can carry an energy number — 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 or 13 — and every day has the same fixed budget of 13. The meter reports what the day asks and what is left; it never tells you what to do about it.

The Energy tab looks backwards, too: which days were heavy, and which weekday keeps being the heaviest. Patterns become visible without anyone scoring you. A day that asked for more than 13 is named as a fact about the day — not about you.

Work apart from home

Every entry files under a category — Todo to begin with, and whatever else your life actually divides into. You never choose one while writing something down: new entries take the default, and re-filing is a single tap in the entry's own screen. Capture is the moment that must not be taxed.

The Energy tab can then answer a question no meter can: where did it all go? Totals per category over the window you pick — a fact about where the energy went, never a share to balance.

Find it without hunting for it

A planner you cannot search is a planner you have to remember. Type two letters into The Plan and NeuRoutine looks through every title, every note, and every step you broke something down into — the inbox first, because something with no day yet is the commonest answer to where did I put that, and then the days nearest today.

Every result says where it lives — in the inbox, today, Fri, Aug 21 — so finding it and knowing when it is are one glance rather than two. The field sits in the same place whether you are looking at the day, the week or the month, because a control that moves is a control you have to look for. Nothing to scroll through, and nothing to hold in your head.

"Not right now" is an answer

A repeating thing can be let go of for one day without breaking the routine or counting as a miss — and a skipped day costs the day nothing and shows up nowhere as a failure. Anything else can move to tomorrow, to the end of the day, or back to the inbox.

Plain words, one per line, nothing hidden behind a swipe, and every one of them reversible. A day that ends with the list let go of says so honestly: "that is a decision, not a failure."

Some days are smaller

One switch on Now says today is a low-energy day, and Now narrows to what you could actually manage — you decide where that line sits, on the same 0-to-13 scale, plus whether unsized things and anything marked High stay regardless.

It is on until tomorrow and then gone, with nothing to remember to undo. Anything with a time on it always stays, because a 14:00 appointment happens whether the day is heavy or not. The Plan keeps the whole day, the meter still counts what the day costs, and nothing is completed, skipped or moved. It is a lens, not a lie.

On the Home Screen, without the noise

The On deck widget shows the one thing to do now, what follows it, and a Done button that works right from the Home Screen. No counts, no lists, no red badges.

On the Lock Screen, Now and next spans two slots and has room for the words; two rings keep the day's two facts apart — how much of the list is done, and how much energy is left — because a day can be four-of-five done and still ask for more than there is. A Control Centre button opens the app straight into capture, cursor already in the field.

Your data stays yours

Entries are real events and reminders in your own Calendar and Reminders — usable in Apple's apps, on every device you already have. The details Apple's apps have no place for — energy, steps, categories, which days a repeat was carried — sync through your private iCloud. No account, no server, nothing collected, and nothing to set up.

The privacy policy is one page, because there is almost nothing to say.

The rest of the argument

Five rooms, one job each

Now is for doing, The Plan is for arranging — the day, and the week and month behind one fixed picker — Inbox holds what has no day yet, Energy looks back, Settings is set once. Nothing moves around on you.

It never interrupts

No notifications, no badges, no nudges about what is still undone — it never even asks for permission to notify. The app shows you your day when you open it, and is silent when you don't.

Predictable on purpose

Weeks always start on Monday, the month grid never changes shape, one colour carries one meaning, and a day can be dragged into your order — with the one rule that the clock never runs backwards.

Made to be spoken to

Siri shortcuts add something, tick something off for the right day, or say how today is going — and full VoiceOver support, Dynamic Type through the accessibility sizes, and honest tap targets are part of the app, not an afterthought.
