Dinner at 17:30, exactly

Meals is a meal planner for iPhone, iPad and Mac, designed neurodivergent-friendly first. Not "sometime this evening" — 17:30. Meals are real events in your own Calendar, at times that hold still.

It records what you ate, if you want it to. It never counts calories against a target, never scores a day, and has no opinion about any of it.

Recipes that tell the truth about eating

A recipe here records texture and temperature alongside the ingredients, because on a hard day that is what actually decides whether food happens. And for the worst days there is the safe-food roster: the things that always work, one tap away, no browsing required.

Cooking, one step at a time

When it is time to cook, the recipe walks you through a single step per screen. No video, no audio, nothing moving in the corner of your eye. The grocery list leaves the app as Reminders grouped by aisle, in the order you walk the shop.

It records. It never scores.

There are no calorie targets, no weight goals, no streaks, and no number the app compares you against. Logging a meal is one button: the block is marked done, and the nutrition goes to Apple Health if you switched that on. An app that reads what somebody ate and has an opinion about it is not a feature we want to build.

Also true

Quiet by default

Meals can give you a heads-up before a meal block starts, because a warning makes a transition easier. It is off until you switch it on, and the app never asks for notification permission before that.

Dark by default

A planner that opens white at 17:30 in a dim kitchen is a flash to the face, at the exact moment someone came to it for help. Meals opens dark. The choice is yours to change, and it is remembered.

Your data stays yours

Meal blocks live in your Calendar and groceries in your Reminders, synced by your own iCloud. Recipes, textures and the safe-food roster stay on your device. No account, no server, nothing collected.

Nothing moves uninvited

Static screens, no autoplaying anything, and motion follows your Reduce Motion setting. Predictability is the whole point of planning meals in the first place.
